# Find collaborators or request review

Use [OSRI Q&A](https://github.com/orgs/Open-Source-Research-Initiative/discussions/new?category=q-a). Sign in to post. You may use your preferred language.

## Find collaborators or offer help

Use a title such as **Find collaborators: [question]** or **Offer help: [field or task]**. Include:

- The question and project link, if one exists.
- A small task or the work you can help with.
- The subject or methods knowledge needed.
- A useful first result and any access or resource limits.

Ask a maintainer to confirm the human lead and next task. Record agreed roles in the project's `contributions.md`. Discuss authorship as the work develops; confirm it before submission.

## Request review

Open [Q&A](https://github.com/orgs/Open-Source-Research-Initiative/discussions/new?category=q-a) with **Review request: [project or result]**. Or open an issue in the research project and link the discussion. Include:

- The exact draft, result, or repository version to examine.
- The question or claim and links to supporting evidence.
- The checks already performed, their results, and unresolved limits.
- The domain or methods expertise needed and the decision requested.

Ask a maintainer to confirm the reviewer and next action in the discussion or linked issue. Keep review marked as pending until it occurs. If no reviewer is assigned, ask [Cole / @ajhcs](https://github.com/ajhcs) for help. Reviewer availability is not guaranteed.

Record the reviewer, findings, resulting changes, and remaining review needs with the project. Distinguish self-review, another person's review, agent checks, and venue peer review. An agent's review is assistance. The human lead authorizes publication of the reviewed version.

For private matters, [email Cole](mailto:cole.lyons@pennmedicine.upenn.edu). Agree on delivery and storage before sending confidential material.
