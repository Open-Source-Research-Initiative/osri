## Methods and existing overlap

DIGITAL HEALTH expects a critical, comprehensive Review and clear methods for research articles. Deegan et al.'s [*A scoping review of frameworks evaluating digital health applications*](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/20552076251315297) (2025; DOI 10.1177/20552076251315297) already connects evaluation frameworks with digital-health and VR deployment. Medical XR should show what its capability-to-task chronology and sustainability evidence add.

Frontiers specifies transparent review methods, appropriate reporting guidance and study flow. Its Review and Systematic Review types exclude unpublished material; a manuscript combining literature synthesis with original technology-history analysis needs article-type clarification. Morgan, Patel and Campbell's [*Practical considerations of clinical XR (AR/VR) deployments*](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/virtual-reality/articles/10.3389/frvir.2025.1517402/full) (2025; DOI 10.3389/frvir.2025.1517402) already addresses institutional, technical and reimbursement challenges. A new map needs traceable evidence and a distinct contribution beyond that perspective.

JMIR requires applicable reporting checklists and transparent review methods; registration is recommended. Its [selection criteria](https://www.jmir.org/publishing-policies/criteria-for-selection-of-manuscripts) require valid data and supported conclusions. Sarkar et al.'s [*Barriers and Facilitators to the Implementation of Virtual Reality as a Pain Management Modality in Academic, Community, and Safety-Net Settings: Qualitative Analysis*](https://www.jmir.org/2021/9/e26623) (2021; DOI 10.2196/26623) overlaps on workflow, resources and reimbursement.

Additional overlap crosses venue boundaries: Elser et al.'s [chronic-pain implementation scoping review](https://xr.jmir.org/2024/1/e53129) (2024; DOI 10.2196/53129) appeared in **JMIR XR and Spatial Computing**, a different journal. Kouijzer et al.'s [*Implementation of virtual reality in healthcare: a scoping review on the implementation process of virtual reality in various healthcare settings*](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s43058-023-00442-2) (2023; DOI 10.1186/s43058-023-00442-2) appeared in *Implementation Science Communications*. These examples are a bounded overlap check, not a complete novelty search.

## AI disclosure and policy fit

Under [Sage's journal policy](https://www.sagepub.com/journals/publication-ethics-policies/artificial-intelligence-policy), generative assistance affecting research or content requires disclosure and source verification; ordinary language assistance is exempt. AI cannot be an author. Prohibited uses include analysis of experiences and themes. Cole should clarify how the inherited agent-assisted evidence coding fits before choosing DIGITAL HEALTH. The book-author AI policy is a separate policy and was not used here.

[Frontiers' policy](https://www.frontiersin.org/guidelines/policies-and-publication-ethics) calls for disclosure in acknowledgments and Methods when applicable, including tool, version, model and source. Human authors must check accuracy and originality. AI authorship is excluded; supplying prompts and outputs is encouraged.

[JMIR's policy, updated 23 October 2026](https://support.jmir.org/hc/en-us/articles/13387268671771-JMIR-Publications-Editorial-Policy-on-the-use-of-generative-AI-during-manuscript-preparation), uses a dedicated question at submission and every resubmission. The published AI disclosure has its own section; it should not be duplicated in acknowledgments. The policy requests retained prompts/responses and manuscript-preparation conversations as a Multimedia Appendix. Methods still explain AI used in the study. Generated text should be identified in a blockquote, textbox or figure. This newer process changes the disclosure location described in the [2023 editorial](https://www.jmir.org/2023/1/e51584).
