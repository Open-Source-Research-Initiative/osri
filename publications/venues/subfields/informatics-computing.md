# Computer science, AI, data and research software

Free peer-reviewed routes with a compatible AI policy and core editorial standards documented. Check current author instructions before submission.

## Journal of Open Source Software

**Fit:** Peer-reviewed papers about mature research software; requires more than six months of active public development and demonstrated research use, which may be by the developers. External community adoption is a positive signal, not mandatory. Pre-trained models, notebooks, thin API clients and one-off tools are outside scope. Humans must make core design decisions and validate AI-assisted work.

**Submission fee:** none. **Publication fee:** none.

**Conditions and other costs:** No fees to submit or publish. Requires mature, research-used software under an OSI-approved license, tests, documentation and more than six months of active public development. A major contributor submits, participates in public review and archives a tagged release with a DOI. AI assistance requires disclosure and human validation; AI-mediated author/editor or reviewer conversation is prohibited except translation. Models, notebooks, thin API clients and one-off analysis tools are outside scope.
No submission or publication fees. Software must meet research-impact, feature-completeness and open-development gates, including more than six months of active public development under current pre-review criteria. Major-contributor authors participate through GitHub; archive the reviewed release with a DOI using a free archive. Disclose AI tools, use and human validation. AI use in conversational interactions between authors and editors/reviewers is prohibited except translation. Human authors must make core design decisions and take responsibility.
Journal states no fees for manuscript processing or publishing and zero publication fees. Research software must satisfy submission requirements. None in the journal submission/publication policy. Repository hosting must permit free public access; free supported archive choices exist. Existing software development expenses are not journal charges.

**AI policy:** Most aspects of submission including software creation, review, and paper authoring may use generative AI; humans review and validate outputs and make core design decisions.

**Disclosure (required):** Include an AI usage disclosure statement identifying tools/models and versions, where used, nature and scope of assistance, and confirmation that humans reviewed, edited and validated outputs and made core design decisions.

## Transactions on Machine Learning Research

**Fit:** Original ML studies, methods, reproducibility and applications yielding insight into learning methods. Survey submissions are excluded from September 1, 2026, despite a Survey Certification still appearing in the editorial page. Archival conference-paper extensions are excluded; preprints and expressly non-archival workshop overlap can be eligible. Current official pages differ in specificity on LLM use: the editorial policy permits assistive tools, while the FAQ expects human-sourced ideas, claims and results and requires first-page disclosure.

**Submission fee:** none. **Publication fee:** none.

**Conditions and other costs:** No author fees. Every author needs an active OpenReview profile and sufficient annual submission quota, including for desk-rejected submissions. Length must be justified. No surveys from September 1, 2026; no expanded archival conference papers. The editorial LLM policy allows assistive use with human responsibility; the FAQ additionally expects human-sourced ideas/claims/results and first-page disclosure. Assess both against the actual workflow. Journal-to-conference presentation and attendance are separate. None for journal submission and publication under the blanket no-author-fee policy. Journal-to-conference travel or registration is outside this route and is not required to publish.

**AI policy:** TMLR permits general-purpose LLM assistance and explicitly permits help writing submissions. Human authors must supply the ideas, claims and results, verify all content and remain responsible. Use AI for assistance with research and analytical code rather than to originate unverified scientific claims or results; disclose its use on the first page. Permission for analytical code is an interpretation of the broad general-purpose assistance rule, rather than a code-specific clause.

**Disclosure (required):** Mention use of the LLM explicitly in a footnote on the first page of the submission, as required by the author FAQ. The editorial policy does not repeat this requirement; follow the more specific FAQ.
