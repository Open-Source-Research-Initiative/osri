# OSRI: first researchers and contributors

Prepared November 16, 2026. Day 1 is the day we begin the launch work. This is a proposed 30-day launch plan; no invitations, public posts, or partnership requests have been sent as part of preparing it.

## Outcome and ownership

Use the venue directory to help researchers make a real publication decision, then invite interested users to complete one useful contribution. Start with personal introductions and public sharing. Approach libraries and other organizations after we have evidence of use and maintenance.

Codex owns preparation: the launch backlog, onboarding improvements, source-check packets, demonstration materials, outreach drafts, review support, and weekly reporting during our working sessions. Cole provides the personal introductions and public voice, helps interpret researcher feedback, and makes human maintainer acceptance decisions. Codex does not run continuously between chats or independently monitor replies; incoming responses need to be brought into a working session or handled by Cole.

We will use a work queue rather than assign Cole an arbitrary weekly-hours budget. Keep no more than five researchers in the next feedback batch and three contributor tasks actively claimed at once. These are manageable starting limits, not restrictions on further preparation.

### Day-30 targets

| Outcome | Target | What counts |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Qualified personal invitations | 20 | Individual invitations through existing relationships or referrals to people with a current or planned manuscript |
| Researchers completing useful directory use | 10 | A distinct external researcher builds a shortlist and reports at least one plausible venue, with a reason and the relevant fee/eligibility conditions |
| First external contributors | 3 | Three distinct humans complete useful, agreed tasks that a human maintainer accepts |
| Accepted tasks | 5 | Source checks, useful corrections, scope assessments, documentation or usability findings with retained evidence |
| Returning contributors | 1 | At least one of those humans completes a second accepted task |
| Shareable examples | 2 | Consented real-use examples, or public-source guides clearly distinguished from actual user outcomes |

These targets are hypotheses for a small launch, not predicted conversion rates. Agent runs, our browser checks, stars, likes, and invitations alone do not count as researcher use or external contribution.

## Starting point and initial audience

The directory currently has 57 verified free, peer-reviewed routes with core standards documented, an expanded set of 73 free routes without medium/high concerns, and 146 AI-compatible records available for inspection. Its comparison and shareable URLs already support the pilot. All routes retain article-type, language, AI and other conditions; author-free publication can include a subscription route that restricts reader access. The [directory overview](https://github.com/Open-Source-Research-Initiative/free-publication-directory/blob/main/README.md) and [quality findings](https://github.com/Open-Source-Research-Initiative/free-publication-directory/blob/main/docs/quality-review.md) document this baseline.

Live GitHub inspection on November 16 found no open issues in either the directory or hub. The contribution register has no accepted tasks recorded. The directory contribution guide still points readers toward older technical research-file workflows. GitHub repository traffic is not a measure of directory website use, and there is no reliable website-use baseline yet. Do not describe these observations as proof that nobody has visited or helped.

Start with two audiences:

- Public-health and health-services researchers, especially students and early-career researchers choosing a venue for existing work. The default list currently includes six public-health and five health-systems routes; these field counts overlap.
- Education and behavioral/social researchers with a current or planned manuscript. Education and psychology each currently have six routes in the default list; counts overlap.

Use public research topics or participant-selected papers for demonstrations. A full unpublished manuscript is unnecessary. Software researchers can join, but the computing default currently has only two routes and should not be presented as broad coverage of computer science. Several fields currently have no routes in the stricter default.

The central invitation is: find a suitable venue with free submission and a free publication route, inspect its conditions and AI-disclosure policy, and compare alternatives. Make the request specific: try it on one paper or verify one source. Explain OSRI's human-led, Agentic-Powered-Research model accurately; do not promise publication, universal AI permission, or scientific certification.

## Four-week execution

| When | Codex prepares and supports | Cole's introductions and decisions | Completion evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Days 1–3 | Update contribution instructions; prepare simple issue forms, directory feedback links, six starter tasks, a 60–90 second demonstration, and two field-specific starting links | Supply the first five names or introductions; review the invitation and public wording | A newcomer can report a source or claim a task without editing JSON or installing tools; all links work |
| Days 4–7 | Prepare personalized notes for the first five invitations; run a researcher-use checklist; summarize observed problems and prepare fixes | Make the introductions and join or relay the first feedback conversations | Three completed trials, or documented reasons people declined; a prioritized list of actual obstacles |
| Week 2 | Prepare five more invitations and referral requests; publish-ready professional-network and OSRI announcement drafts; pair interested people with a starter task | Send or approve the public posts; accept useful work after reviewing its evidence | Five completed researcher uses cumulatively; first accepted external task; one small improvement shipped from feedback |
| Week 3 | Prepare ten further invitations through referrals; draft one contributor-recruitment post for an eligible community; offer each contributor a relevant second task | Make introductions or referrals and review acceptance decisions | Ten completed researcher uses targeted; three contributors engaged; useful changes and remaining gaps documented |
| Week 4 | Follow up once with willing participants; consolidate source checks; produce two guides/use cases and a candid launch report | Approve public credit, consented examples and the next month's priorities | Five accepted tasks from three people, one repeat contributor targeted, and a decision on readiness for library outreach |

The short demonstration must show: select a field, compare three venues, open submission/publication conditions, and locate AI disclosure instructions. Use a direct directory link as the researcher call to action and a direct starter-task link for prospective contributors.

Each invitation asks for a 10–15 minute trial or a brief asynchronous reply. During a trial, let the researcher attempt the task before explaining the controls. Record whether they found a plausible fit, what made them trust or reject it, and where they needed help. This is product feedback, not evidence that a paper has been submitted or accepted.

## Give contributors a small, supported first task

Turn the six task packets in [STARTER-TASKS.md](STARTER-TASKS.md) into starter issues and offer up to three active claims at once. Each issue needs an official starting source, a 10–30 minute initial timebox, a named human maintainer, a precise output and an acceptance checklist. Label suitable tasks `good first issue`, `help wanted`, and `no-code`; do not assign them to people who have not volunteered.

Provide two entry routes:

1. A short GitHub issue form for a correction, source check, or use report. Ask for the venue, observation, official source URL, check date and relevant conditions. Let the reporter say “unclear.”
2. The existing email contact for someone who does not want a GitHub account. With permission, a maintainer turns their note into a public record. Public credit is optional.

Codex checks the cited material, prepares the proposed patch and verification notes, and helps the newcomer understand the result. Cole makes the human acceptance decision. Material AI assistance is disclosed. A useful check can confirm an existing record; contributors do not need to discover a defect. Access failure must be reported as an access limit, not a false policy or non-indexing claim.

Keep original campaign records intact. Integrate new accepted evidence through a dated follow-up and the normal build/review process; do not simply edit a historical campaign decision or automatically promote a venue because a newcomer submitted a note. Record accepted work in the existing contribution register, using one task record per agreed contribution. Credit does not promise manuscript authorship or a maintainer appointment.

Review the queue twice per week while the launch is active. Aim to acknowledge a submission within two working days, with Cole covering notifications between working sessions. Send a substantive next step rather than a generic thank-you, and offer a second task that matches the contributor's interests.

## Distribution and eventual partnerships

Use channels in this order:

1. **Personal invitations:** start with five people Cole knows; expand through introductions and referrals. Each note names the person's field and asks for one concrete trial. Follow up once after about a week; stop if they decline or do not want further contact.
2. **Public professional sharing:** prepare a founder-reviewed LinkedIn post showing one publication-search problem and its solution, plus an OSRI GitHub announcement with a stable permalink. A second post should report a concrete improvement or invite a specific source-check task. The [LinkedIn sharing guide](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a525301/sharing-articles-or-links?lang=en) supports direct links and a preview; no claims about algorithmic reach are assumed.
3. **Contributor recruitment:** use the current [r/opensource Contributors Wanted thread](https://www.reddit.com/r/opensource/comments/1xs6ok0/contributors_wanted/) after checking its current replacement and rules. Include the source repository, MIT code / CC BY 4.0 original directory material, Python and plain JavaScript, the exact starter tasks, and the human-responsibility/AI-disclosure policy. This is a contributor channel, not evidence of researcher adoption. The verified thread permits young and AI-assisted projects and limits each project to one comment.

Do not launch promotional or survey posts in r/PhD: its [moderator rules announcement](https://www.reddit.com/r/PhD/comments/1jpsney/updated_community_rulestake_a_look/) prohibits them. Do not assume other academic forums permit promotion; verify the specific venue before proposing a post. No blanket posting schedule, unsolicited mailing-list submission, or mass direct-message campaign is planned.

Prepare the partnership pack in week four. Start institutional outreach only after ten completed researcher uses, accepted work from three external contributors, and two useful guides or consented examples are available. If those targets are not met, publish a candid progress report and continue the pilot rather than claim adoption.

Initial prospects for the next phase are [Penn Libraries publishing support](https://www.library.upenn.edu/research-and-scholarship) and [Author Carpentry](https://authorcarpentry.github.io/), which describes researcher/librarian participation in open authoring and publishing training. These are prospective conversations, not existing partners or endorsements. The first request is feedback on usefulness and maintenance requirements; resource-list inclusion or a short demonstration can follow if staff find it valuable.

## Measure outcomes and change course when needed

Use [pilot-log.template.csv](pilot-log.template.csv) as an empty, local working log. Use participant IDs; do not put private contact lists or unpublished research in public repositories. Record the invitation source, completed trial, shortlist URL, reported fit, obstacle and follow-up. Retain declined invitations, unfinished trials and no-fit results so the report shows failures as well as useful shortlists. Use yes/no/unclear for fit and unaided-use fields; a completed trial without a fit does not count toward the ten useful-shortlist target. Record any permission to quote separately and specifically. Pending contribution work belongs in issues; accepted work follows the existing [contribution-register rules](https://github.com/Open-Source-Research-Initiative/osri/blob/main/community/README.md).

Website visitor and conversion totals remain unknown unless a separate measurement system is deliberately added. UTM parameters alone do not measure visits, and the current directory rewrites URL parameters when filters change. For this pilot, ask how people found the directory and use completed trials and contributed evidence as the primary measures. Do not add an analytics service merely to manufacture a launch dashboard.

Use these checkpoints:

- Fewer than three willing trials after ten well-targeted invitations: revise the audience or invitation and ask willing contacts what made the request unhelpful before sending the next batch.
- Fewer than four of the first five testers can find a plausible route without explanation: prioritize the observed coverage or usability problem before expanding public sharing.
- Several people volunteer but fewer than two finish: shorten the task, improve the starting source and offer a brief paired start.
- First tasks finish but nobody returns within two weeks: ask what work would interest them and offer one specific next task.

The weekly report contains counts, evidence links, the main obstacle, the change shipped, and the next experiment. Record submissions, acceptances and publications only if they actually occur and the researcher permits sharing; they are not 30-day launch success requirements.

## Immediate work package

The first implementation package is the contribution-path repair, six starter issues, two field-specific demonstration links, one short demonstration, personalized invitation drafts and a public-post draft. The task packets and empty outcome log are prepared alongside this plan. Source and current channel rules have been inspected; public posting requires another current-rules check at the time of use.

Budget default: no paid advertising, incentives or new subscription services. LinkedIn is the proposed professional-network channel, subject to Cole having an account and wanting to use it. The sequence remains personal introductions and public sharing, followed by evidence-based partnership outreach.
